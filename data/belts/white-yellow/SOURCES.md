**Status: signed off for this demo** (10 Nov 2026). Official ITF wording quotes are frozen character for character.

## How the sources were used

Accuracy comes first. The official ITF meanings and fixed formulas (the Chon-Ji meaning, the white and yellow belt meanings, the list of tenets and the student oath) are **quoted word for word** from the official ITF sources below, marked "Official ITF wording:" in the notes. Each was copied from the source text, not typed from memory. A kid-friendly "In simple words:" line sits underneath as an extra; it never replaces the official wording. Everything else in the notes is plain kid-level wording, checked against the sources.

## Disagreements / check with your instructor

1. **How Chon-Ji ends. Resolved:** the **left** foot goes back to parallel ready stance, per the official ITF wording (ITF-A), confirmed by Debbie (10 Nov 2026). Some secondary sources (WIKI, NZ-CJ) say the right foot. The app no longer shows a "check with your instructor" tag here.
2. **Student oath wording.** NZ-HB: "I shall observe the **tenets** of Taekwon-Do / respect the instructor and **seniors** / never misuse Taekwon-Do / be a champion of freedom and justice / **build** a more peaceful world." ITF-FI: "observe the **rules** of Taekwon-Do", "instructor and **senior belts**", "**help to build** a more peaceful world." The notes use the NZ-HB version. Use your club's exact words.
3. **Name of the Chon-Ji L-stance block.** WIKI: "middle inner forearm **side** block" (an palmok kaunde yop makgi). ITF-A and NZ-CJ: "middle block with the inner forearm". ITF-FI: "An Palmok Kaunde Makgi". Same move, different names.
4. **What "Ji" means.** Most sources: "the Heaven the Earth". WIKI adds that the Hanja reading points to Heaven Lake (Cheonji) on Mount Paektu, and that 19 moves may refer to the three-colour taegeuk (8 + 8 + 3). These extra ideas are not in the notes.
5. **"Two similar parts" vs "two simple parts".** NZ-HB, NZ-CJ, ITF-A: similar. ITF-FI: simple. The notes quote "two similar parts".
6. **Saju Makgi technique.** NZ-HB and ITF-FI: knife-hand low block + inner forearm middle block. Some clubs teach a forearm low block version. Check.
7. **Saju Jirugi start.** ITF-FI starts by stepping forward with the right foot and punching. Some clubs start by stepping back. The notes avoid the exact first step. Check.
8. **Where the yellow belt meaning is tested.** The plan puts it in grading 2 (9th to 8th kup). NZ-HB lists it under the 8th to 7th kup theory. Check your club's syllabus.
9. **Spelling.** dul/dool, kup/gup, tul/teul, do jang/dojang, do bok/dobok, ap kumchi/apkumchi, sabum nim/sabeomnim. Romanisation varies between sources.
10. **Instructor titles.** NZ-HB: boosabum nim for 1st to 3rd dan, sabum nim for 4th to 6th dan. Many clubs say sabum nim for any instructor.
11. **Stance and technique details** (foot width, knee bend, weight split such as L-stance "most weight on the back leg", first two knuckles) are written from general teaching and were **not** checked against a source. Check all of them.
12. **Sitting stance and turning** are on the NZ 9th kup syllabus but were not in Debbie's scope list. Keep or drop them.
13. **Counting spellings.** Notes: hana, dul, set, net, dasot, yosot, ilgop, yodul, ahop, yol. NZ-HB: **dool**. ITF-FI: **dol**, **tasot**, **ilgob**. Same Korean words, different romanisation. Use the club's spelling.
14. **Saju Jirugi and Saju Makgi footwork.** ITF-FI: Saju Jirugi starts by stepping **forward** with the right foot and punching. In Saju Makgi each direction starts by going **back** into the low block, then stepping **forward** for the side block. The notes leave out the exact footwork. Confirm the start and the footwork, and whether the low block is knife-hand or forearm.
15. **Kihap on Chon-Ji move 17. Resolved:** no kihap in Chon-Ji, per Debbie (10 Nov 2026). ITF-A marks one after move 17; WIKI and NZ-CJ don't. "Kihap" stays in the notes only as a dojang word (yell).
16. **Block names in the notes.** The Chon-Ji low block is a "low **outer** forearm block" in WIKI and NZ-CJ. The notes just say "forearm low block" (the NZ syllabus name). The inner forearm note now uses "an palmok yop makgi" in the title, body and quiz. Confirm which names the club tests.
17. **Heights are unsourced.** High = eye level, middle = shoulder level, low = belt level is general teaching and not in the sources checked.
18. **Unsourced stance and strike details.** None of these were checked against a source: attention stance "toes out a little, like a V" (ITF-FI says feet at 45 degrees); walking stance "back leg straight"; L-stance "most weight on the back leg" (ITF-FI: about 70/30) and the letter-L shape; sitting stance "like sitting on a horse"; front punch "first two big knuckles" and "other hand pulls back to your hip"; front snap kick how-to and "pull your toes back"; kihap "short, loud, from your belly"; forearm low block "down and out"; knife-hand "little-finger edge".
19. **Stepping spellings.** Notes and NZ-HB: nagagi / duruogi. ITF-FI: nakagi / durogi.
20. **Not covered.** The NZ 9th kup syllabus also lists the forearm guarding block, side rising kick, side front snap kick, turning kick, flat fingertip thrust and three-step sparring. These were outside the written scope. If the club tests them at this grading, the notes have a gap.
21. **"Our club does ITF Taekwon-Do"** (in the ITF note). Confirm this is true for the kids' club.
22. **Official ITF wording vs. "In simple words".** The white belt, yellow belt and Chon-Ji meanings, the tenets list and the oath are quoted exactly. The "In simple words" lines under them are kid versions written for this app. Check the instructor is happy with them.

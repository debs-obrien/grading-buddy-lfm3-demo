> Scope: ITF Taekwon-Do only (not World Taekwondo). Grading 1 = 10th kup to 9th kup. Grading 2 = 9th kup to 8th kup.

> Format: every `###` heading is one note. `Tags:` help search. `Quiz:` and `Answer:` are used by Quiz mode. Quiz mode shows the answer plus two wrong options: from `Wrong:` (wrong options separated by `|`) or, if a note has no `Wrong:`, from the answers of other notes with the same `Kind:`. After a tap the quiz shows `Why:` if the note has one, otherwise the note's first sentence or two. The text under them is what the app shows the model.

## Grading 1: 10th kup to 9th kup

### What the word Taekwon-Do means
Tags: name, word, meaning, foot, fist, way, tae, kwon, do
Grading: 1
Quiz: What do the three parts of the word Taekwon-Do mean?
Answer: Tae is foot, Kwon is fist or hand, Do is the way.
Why: Tae means foot (kicking). Kwon means fist or hand (punching). Do means the way.
Wrong: Tae is hand, Kwon is foot, Do is the belt. | Tae is jump, Kwon is kick, Do is the dojang.

Taekwon-Do is made of three little words. Tae means foot (kicking). Kwon means fist or hand (punching). Do means the way, like a way of living and learning.

### Who started Taekwon-Do
Tags: founder, started, invented, made, created, general, choi, hong hi, who
Grading: 1
Quiz: Who is the founder of Taekwon-Do?
Answer: General Choi Hong Hi.
Wrong: Bruce Lee. | King Sejong.

The founder of Taekwon-Do is General Choi Hong Hi. He was a general in the Korean army. He was born in 1918 and died in 2002.

### When Taekwon-Do got its name
Tags: name, date, 1955, history, birthday, when
Grading: 1
Quiz: In which year was Taekwon-Do given its name?
Answer: 1955.
Wrong: 1966. | 1918.

General Choi gave Taekwon-Do its name on 11 April 1955. You can think of 11 April as Taekwon-Do's name day.

### What ITF means
Tags: itf, international, federation, 1966, organisation
Grading: 1
Quiz: What do the letters ITF stand for?
Answer: International Taekwon-Do Federation.
Wrong: International Training Federation. | Indoor Taekwon-Do Fighting.

ITF stands for International Taekwon-Do Federation. General Choi started it in Seoul, Korea, on 22 March 1966. Our club does ITF Taekwon-Do. World Taekwondo (WT) is a different style with different patterns, so this app only covers ITF.

### What the white belt means
Tags: white, belt, colour, meaning, beginner, 10th kup
Grading: 1
Quiz: What does the white belt mean?
Answer: Innocence, as that of a beginning student who has no previous knowledge of Taekwon-Do.
Wrong: Danger, cautioning the student to exercise control and warning the opponent to stay away. | The plant's growth as the Taekwon-Do skill begins to develop.
Why: In simple words: everyone starts with a white belt. You are a beginner, just starting to learn.

Official ITF wording: "White signifies innocence, as that of a beginning student who has no previous knowledge of Taekwon-Do."
In simple words: everyone starts with a white belt. You are a beginner, just starting to learn, with lots to learn.

### The five tenets
Tags: tenets, five, rules, values, list, courtesy, integrity, perseverance, self-control, indomitable spirit
Grading: 1
Quiz: Can you name the five tenets of Taekwon-Do?
Answer: Courtesy, Integrity, Perseverance, Self-Control and Indomitable Spirit.
Wrong: Speed, power, kicking, punching and jumping. | Strength, balance, stretching, speed and winning.

Official ITF wording: "1. Courtesy. 2. Integrity. 3. Perseverance. 4. Self-Control. 5. Indomitable Spirit."
In simple words: the tenets are the five big ideas every Taekwon-Do student tries to live by.

### Courtesy
Tags: courtesy, polite, manners, kind, respect, first tenet
Grading: 1

Courtesy is the first tenet. It means being polite and kind: saying please and thank you, bowing, listening to your instructor and being nice to other students.

### Integrity
Tags: integrity, honest, truth, right, wrong, second tenet
Grading: 1
Quiz: What does integrity mean?
Answer: Being honest and doing the right thing.
Wrong: Never giving up. | Being the strongest in the class.

Integrity is the second tenet. It means being honest, knowing what is right and wrong, and doing the right thing even when nobody is watching.

### Perseverance
Tags: perseverance, keep trying, never give up, patience, third tenet
Grading: 1

Perseverance is the third tenet. It means you keep trying, even when something is hard, and you practise again and again until you get better.

### Self-Control
Tags: self-control, self control, calm, temper, careful, fourth tenet
Grading: 1

Self-Control is the fourth tenet. It means you are the boss of your body and your feelings. You stay calm, you do not lose your temper, and you are careful not to hurt your partner.

### Indomitable Spirit
Tags: indomitable spirit, brave, courage, fifth tenet
Grading: 1

Indomitable Spirit is the fifth tenet. It means being brave and having a strong heart: you stand up for what is right and you do not give up, even when you feel scared or tired.

### The student oath
Tags: oath, promise, student oath, i shall, recite
Grading: 1
Quiz: What is the first line of the student oath?
Answer: I shall observe the Tenets of Taekwon-Do
Wrong: I shall always win | I shall kick the highest
Why: The first promise of the oath is "I shall observe the Tenets of Taekwon-Do". In simple words: I will follow the five tenets.

The student oath is five promises we say together in class. Official ITF wording:
1. I shall observe the Tenets of Taekwon-Do
2. I shall respect the Instructor and Seniors.
3. I shall never misuse Taekwon-Do.
4. I shall be a champion of freedom and justice.
5. I shall build a more peaceful world.
Check with your instructor: some ITF groups use slightly different words (for example "help to build a more peaceful world"), so say it the way your club does.

### What the oath promises mean
Tags: oath, meaning, misuse, freedom, justice, peaceful, explain
Grading: 1
Quiz: What does "I shall never misuse Taekwon-Do" mean?
Answer: Never use Taekwon-Do to hurt or bully anyone, only to stay safe.
Wrong: Only use Taekwon-Do in competitions. | Never tell anyone you do Taekwon-Do.

In kid words: follow the five tenets, be respectful to your instructor and the older belts, never use Taekwon-Do to bully or show off, stand up for what is fair, and help make the world a kinder place.

Charyot means attention. When you hear charyot, you stand up straight in attention stance, quiet and still, ready to listen.

Junbi means ready. When you hear junbi, you get into your ready stance and wait for the next command.

### Baro (return)

Baro means return. When you hear baro at the end of an exercise, you go back to your ready position.

### Kihap (yell)

Answer: A loud yell or shout.

Kihap means a yell or shout. A good kihap is short, loud and comes from your belly. It helps you breathe out and put power into a move.

The dojang (do jang) is the training hall, the place where we practise. The dobok (do bok) is the white training uniform we wear.

### Instructor (sabum nim)
Tags: instructor, teacher, sabum nim, boosabum nim, title
Grading: 1

Answer: Sabum nim.

We call the instructor sabum nim. Some clubs use boosabum nim for a 1st to 3rd degree black belt instructor and sabum nim for 4th to 6th degree. Ask your club which one they use.

### High, middle and low
Tags: high, middle, low, nopunde, kaunde, najunde, height, word
Grading: 1
Quiz: What is the Korean word for middle?
Answer: Kaunde.
Why: Middle is kaunde, about shoulder level.
Wrong: Nopunde. | Najunde.

Moves aim at three heights. High is nopunde (about eye level). Middle is kaunde (about shoulder level). Low is najunde (about belt level and below).

### Punch, block and kick words
Tags: punch, block, kick, jirugi, makgi, chagi, word
Grading: 1
Quiz: What is the Korean word for block?
Answer: Makgi.
Wrong: Jirugi. | Chagi.

Three words you hear all the time: jirugi means punch, makgi means block, chagi means kick.

### Attention stance (charyot sogi)
Tags: attention stance, charyot sogi, stance, heels, sogi
Grading: 1
Quiz: What is attention stance called in Korean?
Answer: Charyot sogi.
Kind: stance-korean

Sogi means stance. Attention stance is charyot sogi. Stand tall with your heels together and your toes pointing out a little, like a V. Arms down by your sides, eyes looking forward.

### Parallel stance and parallel ready stance
Tags: parallel stance, narani sogi, parallel ready stance, narani junbi sogi, ready stance, stance
Grading: 1
Quiz: What is parallel ready stance called in Korean?
Answer: Narani junbi sogi.
Kind: stance-korean

Parallel stance is narani sogi. Your feet are about shoulder width apart and both point straight forward, like train tracks. Parallel ready stance is narani junbi sogi: the same feet, with both fists held in front of your belt. Chon-Ji and Saju Jirugi start from parallel ready stance.

### Walking stance (gunnun sogi)
Tags: walking stance, gunnun sogi, stance, front knee, step
Grading: 1
Quiz: What is walking stance called in Korean?
Answer: Gunnun sogi.
Kind: stance-korean

Walking stance is gunnun sogi. One foot is in front, like taking a big step. Bend your front knee, keep your back leg straight, and keep your weight even on both feet. If your left foot is in front it is a left walking stance.

### Stepping forward and backward
Tags: step, forward, backward, nagagi, duruogi, moving
Grading: 1
Quiz: What is the Korean word for stepping forward?
Answer: Nagagi.
Wrong: Duruogi. | Junbi.

Nagagi means stepping forward. Duruogi means stepping backward. We practise stepping in walking stance before we learn patterns.

### Forearm low block (palmok najunde makgi)
Tags: low block, forearm, palmok, najunde makgi, block, outer forearm
Grading: 1
Quiz: What is a forearm low block called in Korean?
Answer: Palmok najunde makgi.
Kind: technique-korean

A forearm low block is palmok najunde makgi. Palmok means forearm. You sweep your forearm down and out to stop a kick or punch aimed at your tummy or legs. It is the first move of Chon-Ji.

### Knife-hand low block (sonkal najunde makgi)
Tags: knife-hand, knifehand, sonkal, low block, najunde makgi, block, saju makgi
Grading: 1
Quiz: What is a knife-hand low block called in Korean?
Answer: Sonkal najunde makgi.
Kind: technique-korean

A knife-hand low block is sonkal najunde makgi. Sonkal means knife-hand: an open hand with the fingers together, blocking with the little-finger edge. It is a low block, used in Saju Makgi.

### Inner forearm side block (an palmok yop makgi)
Tags: inner forearm, an palmok, side block, yop makgi, middle block, block
Grading: 1
Quiz: What is an inner forearm side block called in Korean?
Answer: An palmok yop makgi.
Kind: technique-korean

An inner forearm side block is an palmok yop makgi. An palmok means inner forearm, the thumb side of your arm. You bring it across to block at middle height. It is used in Saju Makgi and in Chon-Ji.

### Front punch (ap jirugi)
Tags: front punch, ap jirugi, forefist, ap joomuk, punch, fist
Grading: 1
Quiz: What part of the hand do you punch with in a front punch?
Answer: The forefist, the first two big knuckles.
Wrong: The fingertips. | The little finger side of the fist.

A front punch is ap jirugi. You punch with the forefist (ap joomuk): the first two big knuckles of your fist. You can punch high (nopunde), middle (kaunde) or low (najunde). The other hand pulls back to your hip.

### Front snap kick (apcha busigi)
Tags: front snap kick, apcha busigi, kick, ball of the foot, ap kumchi
Grading: 1
Quiz: Which part of the foot do you use for a front snap kick?
Answer: The ball of the foot.
Wrong: The heel. | The top of the foot.

A front snap kick is apcha busigi. Lift your knee, snap your foot out and pull it straight back. You hit with the ball of your foot (ap kumchi), so pull your toes back.

### Front rising kick (apcha olligi)
Tags: front rising kick, apcha olligi, kick, stretch, straight leg
Grading: 1
Quiz: What is a front rising kick called in Korean?
Answer: Apcha olligi.
Kind: technique-korean

A front rising kick is apcha olligi. Keep your kicking leg straight and swing it up in front of you, then bring it back down with control. It helps stretch your legs.

### Saju Jirugi (four-direction punch)
Tags: saju jirugi, four direction punch, saju, exercise, punch, directions
Grading: 1
Quiz: What does Saju Jirugi mean?
Answer: Four-direction punch.
Wrong: Four-direction block. | Four-direction kick.

Saju means four directions and jirugi means punch, so Saju Jirugi is the four-direction punch. It is a practice exercise, not a pattern. You start in parallel ready stance, punch in walking stance, then turn with a low block and punch again, until you have faced all four directions. You do it once starting with the right foot and once with the left.

### Saju Makgi (four-direction block)
Tags: saju makgi, four direction block, saju, exercise, block, directions
Grading: 1
Quiz: What does Saju Makgi mean?
Answer: Four-direction block.
Wrong: Four-direction punch. | Four-direction kick.

Saju Makgi is the four-direction block. It is also a practice exercise. In walking stance you do a knife-hand low block, then step forward with an inner forearm middle block, and you turn so you block in all four directions. You do it once on each side.

## Grading 2: 9th kup to 8th kup

### What a pattern (tul) is
Tags: pattern, tul, teul, form, patterns
Grading: 2
Quiz: What is the Korean word for pattern?
Answer: Tul.
Wrong: Makgi. | Dobok.

A pattern is called tul (you may also see it spelled teul). It is a set of moves done in a fixed order, as if fighting pretend opponents. ITF Taekwon-Do has 24 patterns. Chon-Ji is the first one.

### What Chon-Ji means
Tags: chon-ji, chonji, meaning, heaven, earth, pattern
Grading: 2
Quiz: What does Chon-Ji mean?
Answer: The Heaven the Earth.
Wrong: The Sun the Moon. | The Fire the Water.
Why: Chon-Ji means literally "the Heaven the Earth". In simple words: it is the first pattern, about how everything began.

Official ITF wording: "Chon-Ji means literally “the Heaven the Earth”. It is, in the Orient, interpreted as the creation of the world or the beginning of human history, therefore, it is the initial pattern played by the beginner. This pattern consists of two similar parts; one to represent the Heaven and the other the Earth."
In simple words: Chon is Heaven (the sky above) and Ji is Earth (the ground below). It is about how everything began, so it is the first pattern you learn. It has two halves that are alike: one for Heaven and one for Earth.

### How many moves Chon-Ji has
Tags: chon-ji, chonji, how many, moves, movements, 19, nineteen
Grading: 2
Quiz: How many moves are in Chon-Ji?
Answer: 19.
Wrong: 24. | 12.

Chon-Ji has 19 moves. It is the pattern you need for the grading from 9th kup to 8th kup (yellow stripe to yellow belt).

### Chon-Ji start position and diagram
Tags: chon-ji, chonji, start, ready, parallel ready stance, diagram, cross, plus, A, B, C, D
Grading: 2
Quiz: Which stance does Chon-Ji start in?
Answer: Parallel ready stance (narani junbi sogi).
Wrong: Walking stance (gunnun sogi). | Sitting stance (annun sogi).

Chon-Ji starts in parallel ready stance (narani junbi sogi). The floor pattern is a cross, like a plus sign. You stand in the middle facing D (the front). A is to your right, B is to your left and C is behind you.

### Chon-Ji moves 1 to 4
Tags: chon-ji, chonji, moves 1 to 4, first moves, low block, punch, walking stance, B, A
Grading: 2
Quiz: What is the very first move of Chon-Ji?
Answer: Left walking stance to the left with a left forearm low block.
Wrong: Right walking stance to the right with a right forearm low block. | Sitting stance with a middle punch.

Moves 1 to 4:
1. Step the left foot to the left (B) into a left walking stance and do a low block with the left forearm.
2. Step forward with the right foot into a right walking stance and do a middle punch with the right fist.
3. Turn to the right (clockwise) towards A, into a right walking stance, and do a low block with the right forearm.
4. Step forward with the left foot into a left walking stance and do a middle punch with the left fist.

### Chon-Ji moves 5 to 8
Tags: chon-ji, chonji, moves 5 to 8, low block, punch, walking stance, D, C
Grading: 2
Quiz: In Chon-Ji, which block do you do in moves 5 and 7?
Answer: A forearm low block in walking stance.
Wrong: A knife-hand low block in sitting stance. | An inner forearm block in L-stance.

Moves 5 to 8:
5. Turn to face the front (D) with the left foot, into a left walking stance, and do a low block with the left forearm.
6. Step forward with the right foot into a right walking stance and do a middle punch with the right fist.
7. Turn to the right (clockwise) to face the back (C), into a right walking stance, and do a low block with the right forearm.
8. Step forward with the left foot into a left walking stance and do a middle punch with the left fist.
That is the end of the first part, the part for Heaven.

### Chon-Ji moves 9 to 12
Tags: chon-ji, chonji, moves 9 to 12, l-stance, inner forearm block, punch, A, B, second part
Grading: 2
Quiz: Which stance do you use for the block in Chon-Ji move 9?
Answer: L-stance (niunja sogi).
Wrong: Walking stance (gunnun sogi). | Sitting stance (annun sogi).

The second part, the part for Earth, uses L-stance for the blocks.
9. Move the left foot towards A into a right L-stance and do a middle block with the left inner forearm.
10. Step forward with the right foot into a right walking stance and do a middle punch with the right fist.
11. Turn clockwise towards B into a left L-stance and do a middle block with the right inner forearm.
12. Step forward with the left foot into a left walking stance and do a middle punch with the left fist.

### Chon-Ji moves 13 to 16
Tags: chon-ji, chonji, moves 13 to 16, l-stance, inner forearm block, punch, C, D
Grading: 2
Quiz: In Chon-Ji moves 13 and 15, which part of the arm blocks?
Answer: The inner forearm.
Why: In move 13 you block with the left inner forearm, and in move 15 with the right inner forearm.
Wrong: The knife-hand. | The elbow.

13. Move the left foot towards C into a right L-stance and do a middle block with the left inner forearm.
14. Step forward with the right foot into a right walking stance and do a middle punch with the right fist.
15. Turn clockwise towards D into a left L-stance and do a middle block with the right inner forearm.
16. Step forward with the left foot into a left walking stance and do a middle punch with the left fist.

### Chon-Ji moves 17 to 19 and the end
Tags: chon-ji, chonji, moves 17 to 19, last moves, end, finish, backwards, left foot, ready
Grading: 2
Quiz: How does Chon-Ji end?
Answer: Bring the left foot back to the ready position.
Why: At the end, bring the left foot back to parallel ready stance.
Wrong: With a jumping kick. | With a spin on the spot.

17. Step forward with the right foot into a right walking stance and do a middle punch with the right fist.
18. Step backwards with the right foot into a left walking stance, still facing D, and do a middle punch with the left fist.
19. Step backwards with the left foot into a right walking stance, still facing D, and do a middle punch with the right fist.
End: bring the left foot back to parallel ready stance.

### Punches in Chon-Ji (obverse punch)
Tags: chon-ji, punch, obverse, baro jirugi, reverse, bandae jirugi, same side
Grading: 2
Quiz: In an obverse punch, which hand punches?
Answer: The hand on the same side as the front foot.
Wrong: The hand on the other side from the front foot. | Both hands at the same time.

All the punches in Chon-Ji are middle obverse punches (kaunde baro jirugi). Obverse means you punch with the hand on the same side as your front foot: right foot in front, right fist punches. A reverse punch (bandae jirugi) uses the other hand.

### L-stance (niunja sogi)
Tags: l-stance, l stance, niunja sogi, stance, back leg, chon-ji
Grading: 2
Quiz: What is L-stance called in Korean?
Answer: Niunja sogi.
Kind: stance-korean

L-stance is niunja sogi. Your feet make a shape like the letter L. Most of your weight is on your back leg and both knees are bent. If your right leg is at the back it is called a right L-stance. Chon-Ji uses L-stance for the inner forearm blocks.

### Sitting stance (annun sogi)
Tags: sitting stance, annun sogi, horse, stance, wide
Grading: 2
Quiz: What is sitting stance called in Korean?
Answer: Annun sogi.
Kind: stance-korean

Sitting stance is annun sogi. Your feet are wide apart and point forward, your knees bend out over your feet, and your back stays straight, like sitting on a horse. Your weight is even on both feet.

### What the yellow belt means
Tags: yellow, belt, colour, meaning, earth, plant, roots, 8th kup
Grading: 2
Quiz: What does the yellow belt mean?
Answer: The earth from which a plant sprouts and takes root as the Taekwon-Do foundation is being laid.
Wrong: The Heaven, towards which the plant matures into a towering tree as training in Taekwon-Do progresses. | Danger, cautioning the student to exercise control and warning the opponent to stay away.

Official ITF wording: "Yellow signifies the earth from which a plant sprouts and takes root as the Taekwon-Do foundation is being laid."
In simple words: yellow is the colour of the earth. Like a seed putting down roots before it grows tall, at yellow belt you practise the basics until they are strong.

### Belt order for the first gradings
Tags: belt, order, kup, gup, white, yellow stripe, tag, yellow, 10th, 9th, 8th, after, comes after
Grading: 2
Quiz: Which belt comes after white belt?
Answer: White belt with a yellow stripe (9th kup).
Why: 10th kup is white belt. 9th kup is white belt with a yellow stripe (some clubs call it a tag).
Wrong: Yellow belt with a green stripe. | Black belt.

Coloured belt grades are called kup (also spelled gup) and they count down. 10th kup is white belt. After white belt comes a white belt with a yellow stripe (9th kup; some clubs call it a tag). 8th kup is yellow belt.

### Turning in place and step turning
Tags: turning, turn, spot turning, gujari dolgi, step turning, dolgi
Grading: 2
Quiz: What is turning on the spot called in Korean?
Answer: Gujari dolgi.
Kind: technique-korean

Dolgi means turning. Spot turning (gujari dolgi) means you turn around where you are standing. Step turning means you take a step as you turn. Chon-Ji has turns, so practise turning without losing your balance.
